The seller pathway

Clear information. Fewer surprises.

Selling a property as is generally means offering it in its present condition without agreeing in advance to make repairs or improvements.

Useful information can include the property’s location, approximate size, known issues, occupancy, and your preferred timing.

  • Describe the property accurately.
  • Include clear photographs when available.
  • Identify known material concerns.
  • Review applicable disclosure requirements.
  • Consult a qualified professional before signing.

Seller As-Is Property Agreement

Date: ____________________

Seller: ____________________

Buyer: ____________________

Property: ________________________________________________

Purchase price: ____________________

1. Agreement to sell

Seller agrees to sell, and Buyer agrees to purchase, the property identified above for the agreed purchase price, subject to the terms of this agreement and any additional written terms signed by both parties.

2. As-is condition

The property is offered and accepted in its present, as-is condition. Except as otherwise agreed in writing, Seller is not required to make repairs, improvements, or replacements before closing.

3. Disclosures and inspection

Seller shall provide disclosures required by applicable law and shall not knowingly conceal material defects. Buyer may inspect the property during any inspection period agreed to in writing.

4. Title and closing

Closing shall occur on a mutually agreed date through a title company, attorney, escrow provider, or other authorized settlement professional. Seller shall convey the interest agreed upon, subject to disclosed exceptions and applicable closing requirements.

5. Costs and additional terms

Closing costs, deposits, financing arrangements, possession, and any contingencies shall be allocated according to a separate written agreement or applicable local practice.

6. Entire understanding

Changes to this agreement must be in writing and signed by both parties. This sample is provided for demonstration purposes and should be reviewed by a qualified attorney before use.
